# Review of the route to Dembélé's original field

> **2026-09-15 update (D43):** the domain and local-cover gates described
> below have now passed. See [genus64-cover.md](genus64-cover.md): a validated
> genus-16 domain, exact S3 monodromy and lifted homology b1=128 verify the
> genus-64 arithmetic subgroup. Algebraic equations and the integral target
> comparison remain open. The review below records the state before this work.

Date: 2026-09-14. This review supersedes the route verdicts in D39–D41.
It distinguishes reproduced calculations, published theorems, and proposals.
The objective remains an explicit polynomial for the original field, not just
another field with similar ramification or some matching Frobenius data.

**Finding:** there is a specific, previously untested construction to investigate:
build the higher-level Shimura curve as a curve, then compute its Jacobian's
2-torsion at a good odd prime. A proposed involution quotient reduces the
genus from 136 to 64. Its geometry and algebraic model remain unverified.
This is a research route, not a completed construction or a runtime guarantee.

The smaller F16 detour is definitively solvable. Several earlier statements
that every constructive route was closed were stronger than their evidence.

## 1. What the project has actually accomplished

The durable achievements are substantial. The original Hilbert Hecke computation
has been reproduced over F = Q(zeta_32)^+. The definite class set has size 58;
the weight-two level-one cusp space has dimension 57. The rational Hecke orbit
degrees are 1, 2, 2, 4, 16, 32. The degree-16 orbit gives the desired two
residual systems with coefficient field F256. The characteristic-zero Hecke
field has two primes above 2 of residue degree 8. These are concrete inputs
for a construction, rather than only an existence theorem.

The coefficient-field and descent audits also matter. The degree-16 field's
C8 automorphisms and fixed Q(sqrt(5)) do not produce a usable lower-dimensional
carrier by themselves. The recorded tests find neither CM nor the quadratic
inner twist needed for the proposed shortcut. The Cunningham–Dembélé descent
argument was audited separately; its unresolved issue must not be silently
used to assert an eight-dimensional variety over Q. See
`cunningham-dembele-audit.md` and `stocktake-2026-09-01.md`.

Level raising produced actual alternatives. At the auxiliary norm-2401 prime,
the carrying rational orbit has degree greater than 16. At the prime p above 2,
conductor p gives only the smaller residual systems. Conductor p² gives a
48-dimensional new space, irreducible over Q, containing the target factors
in the recorded T31 and T97 computations. Higher powers produced degree-64
and degree-72 carriers and then twists; they did not reveal a smaller one.
These are valuable negative results with a precise scope.

The project has also located real implementation barriers: costly mixed-sign
periods, unfavorable measured period valuations for the auxiliary-prime
carrier, and the fact that a local toric Galois module is not an algebraic
global field generator. None of those results should be discarded. What needs
revision is the inference from those barriers to universal nonconstructibility.

| Existing conclusion | What survives this review |
|---|---|
| Direct Oda-period calculation is prohibitively expensive in the tested implementation | Supported by its coefficient requirement; not a lower bound for every period algorithm |
| The tested auxiliary-prime carrier is too large for the proposed theta computation | Supported for that carrier and method; does not bound all algebraic invariants of the target |
| Local lambda torsion alone yields the global polynomial | False, as D38 correctly explains |
| The p² carrier has no Mumford uniformization usable by the existing pipeline | Supported; does not exclude arithmetic on its algebraic curve at an odd prime |
| Level-p F16 gives a new nonsolvable field | False; a published theorem identifies the solvable field |
| A few agreeing Hecke polynomials identify the original representation | Insufficient without an all-Hecke argument or another identity certificate |
| Every route to the original field has been exhausted | Not supported |

## 2. The missing paper settles the F16 branch

Dembélé's *An intriguing hyperelliptic Shimura curve quotient of genus 16*
studies exactly the genus-40 discriminant-p Shimura curve X used here.
Theorem 6.4 identifies the normal closures of the 2-torsion fields of its
simple Jacobian factors with the solvable Harbater field, with group
C17 semidirect C16. Thus D40 is closed, and the proposed extra T127 test is
unnecessary. The paper also proves that C = X/<w_D> is hyperelliptic of genus
16 over Q, with an exceptional hyperelliptic involution. Its signatures are
(40; 3^18,16) for X and (16; 2^17,3^9,32) for C. The quartic factors survive
the quotient; the degree-24 factor does not. Explicit equations remain a
separate computational problem in Remark 5.14.
[Dembélé, ANT 14 (2020), revised preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.06772).

Our independent script 62 explains why the old computation missed this:
the surviving quadratic extension has class group C17 under GRH, while the
fallback tested divisibility by 3 and 5. A trace in F16 can have order 3 or 5
while the corresponding determinant-one matrix has order 17. The original
VERDICT lines in `f16_steinberg.out` are preserved as historical output but
must not be used as evidence. The published identification removes the
remaining uncertainty in the computational audit.

This paper therefore supplies both a definitive correction and useful
geometry for the original problem. Its solvable field is not a replacement
for the original nonsolvable target.

## 3. A stronger explanation of the p² congruence

The following is a new local-type argument, with an explicit finite
calculation in `63_p2_sign_type_probe.m`. Its computational result is recorded
in `../data/computed/p2_sign_type_probe.out`: **PASS**, 64.630 CPU seconds
for the revised quotient test. The quotient polynomial is t^10; both target
constituents lie in the sign-supported submodule, and its polynomial equals
the p²-new polynomial modulo 2.

At p the residue field is F2, so GL2(F2) is S3. Its characteristic-zero
cuspidal representation is the one-dimensional sign character: a transposition
acts by -1, and a 3-cycle acts by +1. The sign lattice becomes the trivial
representation on reduction modulo 2.

Use the same definite quaternion algebra and the same 58 ideal classes as
in the original level-one calculation. A sign-valued automorphic function
must vanish integrally on a class whose unit stabilizer contains an odd
permutation. On the other classes its coordinate is free. Reducing these
functions modulo 2 therefore gives a subspace of the level-one functions,
supported on the sign-admissible classes. Reduction commutes with every
Hecke correspondence away from p: each local sign becomes 1. This is an
all-Hecke comparison, rather than an extrapolation from a few traces.

The script computes stabilizer images directly from each left order's unit
group and its residue matrix ring. It finds **48 admissible classes and 10
forbidden classes**. The forbidden indices in this particular computed basis
are 1, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 11, 12, 13, 14. These indices are not intrinsic and
must not be transferred to a separately enumerated class set.

The decisive checks use T31 in that same basis. The package acts on row
functions. Computing only the ten forbidden columns tests stability and
gives the operator on the ten-dimensional quotient. If that quotient's
characteristic polynomial is coprime to each target factor, both generalized
target eigenspaces must lie in the supported subspace. Dividing the banked
raw level-one characteristic polynomial by the quotient polynomial then
checks the supported polynomial against the degree-48 p²-new bank.

The first full-matrix attempt used the wrong column convention and stopped
on its assertion; its output is preserved as
`p2_sign_type_probe_column_attempt.out`. It observed row stability and column
instability. The revised probe follows the package's row-action assembly and
invariant pairing, and uses the quotient test above. This distinction matters
because transposing an integral Brandt matrix can change the reduction lattice.

With these checks passed, the elementary reduction map plus characteristic-zero
eigenvalue lifting explains occurrence of the original maximal ideals in
the sign-type space for the full Hecke algebra. The standard local-type
identification makes this the depth-zero p²-new space. Matching the measured
rank 48 and its irreducible rational Hecke polynomial then identifies the
characteristic-zero carrier. A formal write-up must still state the precise
integral lattice, central-character convention and local-type/Jacquet–Langlands
identification. The calculation is not by itself an explicit algebraic model
of that carrier or an integral comparison with the proposed quotient curve.

## 4. The new construction: a curve before its abelian factor

D39 rejected the degree-48 abelian variety because it is not accessible by
the existing toric-period pipeline. Instead, retain a curve whose Jacobian
contains the required factor. Divisor arithmetic on that curve represents
Jacobian points without constructing a theta embedding of the entire
48-dimensional abelian variety.

The recorded higher-level curve Z has genus 136. Its local division-algebra
level is 1+p_D; its map to X has degree 3. The proposed improvement is to lift
the Atkin–Lehner symmetry and take a quotient Y of genus 64. This is an
inference from local representation theory and the known lower-level
geometry, **not a theorem about a constructed Y in this repository**.

The local quotient D_p^*/(F_p^*(1+p_D)) is S3: the residue units give C3,
and a uniformizer acts by inversion, with central square. The relevant
two-dimensional depth-zero representation has the standard S3 action.
A reflection leaves a one-dimensional invariant subspace. Consequently,
the proposed involution quotient should retain one copy of the degree-48
factor from its two copies upstairs. The old invariant part has dimension
16, giving 16+48 = 64.

The branching prediction is internally consistent:

| Map | Degree | Predicted ramification contribution |
|---|---:|---:|
| X40 to C16 | 2 | 18 |
| Z136 to X40 | 3 | 18 points of index 3: contribution 36 |
| Z136 to Y64 | 2 | 18 |
| Y64 to C16 | 3 | 18 simple branch points and 9 of index 3: contribution 36 |

For example, 2*64-2 = 3*(2*16-2)+18+9*2. Script 64 checks all four
Riemann–Hurwitz identities. These equalities test the proposal but do not
establish existence, descent or the monodromy of the cover. The canonical
model may require care with the field of definition of the deck operations.
The actual congruence subgroup and its normalizer must be constructed.
The predicted orbifold signatures are (64; 2^17,16,32) for Y and
(136; 16^3) for Z. Their co-areas are respectively three and six times
that of C, also checked by script 64. These provide additional concrete
targets for a fundamental-domain implementation.

There is a precise adelic specification to test, rather than only a genus
guess. Qiu's 2025 treatment of this same curve describes the genus-16
quotient using the full local projective division group at p and maximal
level elsewhere, and confirms the old-factor Atkin–Lehner signs.
[Qiu, section 7](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06175v1#S7).
In that description, replace the local group at p by
K = F_p^*(1+p_D) to specify Z, and by the inverse image of a reflection
subgroup C2 in D_p^*/K = S3 to specify Y. Keep the same components away
from p. This identifies which cover and quotient the proposal means.
The standard adelic construction gives an abstract curve for such a level;
what remains to certify here is the claimed connected component, genus and
integral target realization, followed by effective algebraic data.

There are two practical ways to attack the missing model. One is to build
the genus-16 base and then the specified modular cover, using its CM branch
divisors and exact level monodromy. The other is to compute a canonical ring
for Y directly from automorphic forms. Branch locations alone do not select
the cover: torsion in the base's Picard group permits multiple choices.
Neither an arbitrary cubic cover nor the known Harbater polynomial supplies
the desired modular curve.

Y itself cannot be hyperelliptic if it carries the target representation.
Hyperelliptic genus-64 2-torsion is controlled by permutations of at most
130 branch points, whose permutation group has no factor 257 in its order.
SL2(256) has an element of order 257. The useful hyperelliptic object is the
genus-16 base, not the proposed target carrier.

Finally, a degree-two quotient does not give an automatic injection on
2-torsion via pullback. Establish the characteristic-zero factor and then
the appropriate integral residual constituent, or compute the integral
comparison directly. This is a separate check from the genus formula.

## 5. A concrete torsion computation once the model exists

Khuri-Makdisi's divisor algorithms replace Jacobian coordinates by linear
spaces attached to divisors on the curve. This avoids the particular
exponential theta-coordinate enumeration used in the earlier estimates.
It requires algebraic curve data: equations or suitable spaces of sections
and multiplication/evaluation maps. A Brandt matrix alone is not such data.
[Khuri-Makdisi, Linear algebra algorithms for divisors on an algebraic curve](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0105182).

Mascot gives a method to compute and Hensel-lift torsion on a Jacobian at a
good prime different from the torsion prime, isolating the desired part with
a coprime Frobenius factor. The paper explicitly discusses number-field and
coefficient-field extensions. Its genus dependence for torsion lifting is
O(g^(omega+3)); fast group operations do not make the complete lifting
algorithm cubic in genus. Thus genus 64 still needs a benchmark and genus
136 could be much harder. The method addresses the torsion stage after
geometric input exists; it does not produce the curve from Hecke data.
[Mascot, Hensel-lifting torsion points on Jacobians and Galois representations](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.03939).

We can already test the finite-field part without equations. If h is a
degree-eight minimal polynomial of a residual trace and the determinant is
1, the 16-dimensional F2 Frobenius polynomial is

    Psi_h(X) = X^8 h(X + X^(-1)).

Script 64 computes it exactly from the existing T31/T97 data:

| Prime used in the recorded computation | First target factor | Second target factor |
|---|---:|---:|
| Norm 31 | Frobenius order 257 | Frobenius order 255 |
| Norm 97 | Frobenius order 51 | Frobenius order 257 |

Labels in this table refer to the polynomials printed by the script, not
an independently certified pairing of labels between primes. At 31 both
target factors are squarefree and coprime to their cofactors in the proposed
genus-64 Euler polynomial, formed from the four quartic old factors and the
degree-48 new factor. This verifies an isolation condition conditional on
the predicted Jacobian decomposition. The full analogous cofactor test at
97 is still required before choosing it for production.

The attractive prime is a prime above 97 where the chosen target has order
51. Its vectors are defined over F_(97^51), so the necessary unramified
extension degree is 51, not 257. There are 65,535 nonzero vectors and 257
projective lines, each containing 255 nonzero vectors. Frobenius has 1,285
orbits on these vectors when its eigenvalues have order 51.

There is a useful additional simplification. On the isolated two-dimensional
F256 module, Cayley–Hamilton gives

    S = Frobenius + Frobenius^(-1) = a * identity.

The trace a has degree eight, so F2[S] supplies F256 scalar multiplication.
This can organize the vectors into projective lines without implementing
global real-multiplication correspondences on the curve. This statement
applies after isolating the correct module; it does not turn a local
Frobenius computation into a global algebraic function.

Evaluate a globally defined rational function on torsion points, and take
symmetric combinations over the 255 nonzero vectors on each line. A generic
choice should distinguish the 257 lines, but collision checks and coefficient
height measurements are mandatory. The resulting degree-257 polynomial over
F would encode the projective action if the values are distinct and the
recognition is certified. In characteristic 2 the center of SL2(256) is
trivial, so this projective action retains the desired kernel. Taking the
appropriate normal closure over Q then recovers the full target, provided
the residual-identity step has been proved.

## 6. What recent algorithms change, and what they do not

Voight–Willis provide a route from numerical automorphic power series to
equations for compact Shimura curves. This makes a direct canonical-ring
approach credible, but algebraic normalization, numerical precision and
recognition remain real work here.
[Voight–Willis, Computing power series expansions of modular forms](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1205.0045).

There is also an untested replacement for the fundamental-domain computation
that previously stalled: Rickards's PARI/GP implementation. The maintained
`klein` branch is identified by its author as an improved version of the
published implementation. It is worth benchmarking on the exact degree-eight
quaternion algebra before extrapolating from the two-hour Magma attempt.
[Rickards's implementation and build requirements](https://github.com/JamesRickards-Canada/Fundamental-domains-for-Shimura-curves).

Baït–Page's ANTS XVII 2026 algorithm computes cohomology quasi-linearly in
co-area, **given** a fundamental polygon and side pairings. Its available
PARI implementation supports weight two and uses Rickards's code. It can
improve the topological/cohomological stage, but it does not remove the
fundamental-domain prerequisite or recover algebraic curve equations by
itself. This distinction prevents a new misleading feasibility claim.
[Baït–Page paper](https://antsmath.org/ANTSXVII/papers/antsxvii-bait_page-paper.pdf),
[implementation](https://github.com/rayanebait/Cohomology-of-Shimura-curves).

Equationless methods are relevant but not a ready substitute. Mascot's
moduli-friendly Eisenstein construction obtains Jacobian data for modular
curves from elliptic curves with level structure. Transferring it here needs
an equally explicit quaternionic moduli/evaluation description.
[Mascot, Moduli-friendly Eisenstein series](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.14683).
The August 2026 equationless quadratic-Chabauty work concerns non-split
Cartan modular curves; it supplies no drop-in implementation for this
degree-eight quaternionic example.
[Hashimoto–Lido–Lombardo–Mascot–Parent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10919).

Higher-dimensional cohomology is a fallback research direction, not a
competing concrete construction at present. Explicit surface representations
have been computed in other cases, but the family-of-curves approach itself
reports formidable division-polynomial complexity. No matching surface for
this target has been identified in this review.
[Mascot's surface computation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.05885),
[families-of-curves work](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04701).

## 7. Estimates and identity claims that need stricter boundaries

The old target-height note must not be used to select precision. Containment
of wild inertia in a group of order 256 does not prove that inertia equals
that group. The resulting asserted splitting pattern over 2 is unsupported;
the displayed different bound also fails to account correctly for the
ramification of the base. Its extrapolated root-discriminant and height
conclusions are therefore not certificates.

Use the actual local representation and Serre's published analysis for
ramification bounds. The supplement gives a root-discriminant bound below
55.4 for the original normal closure. Such a bound is not a coefficient-height
bound for a chosen generator, and still less for every torsion invariant.
[Original paper and Serre supplement](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0811.4379).

Likewise, large valuations or heights of a chosen carrier do not force all
generators of its residual field to be large. They do justify abandoning
that particular invariant when measured costs are too high. Preserve the
calibrations; remove claims that they exclude arbitrary algebraic functions.

A final polynomial needs more than the right degree, Galois group and
ramification support. The desired field is a particular residual kernel.
An all-Hecke comparison with the original representation, followed through
the integral geometry, provides a path to identity. A finite list of
Frobenius matches is useful error detection but is not automatically that
proof. Auxiliary-prime lottery probabilities and extrapolated heights should
remain explicitly heuristic.

## 8. Recommended next gates

1. **Formalize the sign-type certificate.** Script 63 passes. Write the
   local-type and lattice argument precisely, including its integral
   comparison with the eventual geometric realization. This is more
   informative than another large Hecke-prime scan.
2. **Verify the proposed quotient.** Construct the level subgroup and lifted
   normalizer action. Check connectedness, field of definition, genus 64,
   and the degree-48 factor in characteristic zero. If the quotient fails,
   retain genus 136 as the fallback; do not pretend the smaller genus is proved.
3. **Benchmark the geometric input.** Try Rickards's implementation on X40,
   saving a verified polygon and side pairings. Then induce the prescribed
   level cover and test the quotient action. Put a measured time/memory cap
   on this attempt; a polygon alone is an intermediate result.
4. **Obtain algebraic curve data.** Choose between the genus-16 base with its
   exact modular cover and direct automorphic canonical-ring computation.
   Require a recognized model or verified Makdisi data, with good reduction
   at the chosen odd prime. This is the principal unsolved construction gate.
5. **Benchmark torsion, then recognize.** First isolate and lift a few target
   points at 97, check the full Euler cofactor condition and scalar action,
   and measure invariant heights. Only then enumerate lines and allocate
   precision for a polynomial.
6. **Certify the field.** Prove recognition and the residual identity;
   verify separability, projective action, normal closure and ramification.

The user requested a thorough review and a search for a new route. This
review identifies one and supplies cheap checks for parts of it. It does
not authorize a claim that the hard geometric gate has been passed. No
explicit polynomial for Dembélé's field has been constructed.
