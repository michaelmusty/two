# Quadratic generation and CM jet normalization (D46)

Date: 2026-09-15. This advances the general canonical-model route after
D45. **Exact:** gonality is at least four, so the canonical ideal is
generated by 91 quadrics. **Numerical:** a fixed, real, period-normalized
set of those equations has been computed. Their coefficients have not
been recognized exactly; there is still no model over a number field.

## Exact quadratic-generation gate

Script 76 constructs a minor of D45's verified stable graph. Its seven
branch sets, in the published graph labels, are

    {1,5,12,24}, {2,4,11,23}, {3,6,10,15,19},
    {7,16,20,25,28}, {8,17,21,26,30},
    {9,18,22,27}, {13,14,29}.

They are disjoint, cover all 30 vertices, and each induces a connected
subgraph. Joining two branch sets when an original edge joins them gives
a seven-vertex, sixteen-edge minor with degree sequence

    5,5,5,5,4,4,4.

This is a directly checked finite witness, independent of the randomized
search that found it. Every graph of treewidth k is k-degenerate, so this
minor has treewidth at least four. Treewidth is minor-monotone; hence the
original graph has treewidth at least four as well. By
[van Dobben de Bruyn–Gijswijt, Treewidth is a lower bound on graph gonality](https://alco.centre-mersenne.org/articles/10.5802/alco.124/),
the same lower bound holds for the gonality of any metric realization.
Baker specialization then gives gonality(C)>=4.

Thus C is neither hyperelliptic nor trigonal. Its genus is 16, so it is
not a smooth plane quintic, whose genus would be six. Petri's theorem
therefore says that the homogeneous canonical ideal is generated by
quadrics; see
[Green–Lazarsfeld, A simple proof of Petri's theorem on canonical curves](https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/robert.lazarsfeld/Reprints/Green.Laz.Simple.Pf.Petri.pdf).
The dimension of this quadratic space is

    dim Sym^2 H^0(K) - h^0(2K) = 136 - 45 = 91.

This justifies the target of the D44/D46 numerical calculation. It does
not certify that floating-point equations are exact generators.

There is also an exact finite jet bound. A nonzero section of 2K has a
zero divisor of degree 4g-4=60. Therefore a quadratic expression in
holomorphic differentials that vanishes through local order 60 must
vanish identically. **Coefficients numbered 0 through 60 suffice** to
determine all canonical quadrics when the differential coefficients are
known exactly. This statement uses a coarse curve parameter, not an
upstairs orbifold parameter. At the order-32 CM point, use u=(w/R)^32.

## Removing the arbitrary basis and common period scale

Write the differential coefficients as a row vector of series H(u).
The numerical 16-by-16 matrix of coefficients of u^0,...,u^15 is
invertible, with condition number about 555. Multiplying by its inverse
gives a uniquely normalized basis

    H_i(u) = u^i + sum_(n>=16) a_(n,i) u^n,  0<=i<16.

Let alpha=3*a_(16,15), which is nonzero in the numerical data. Define

    v=alpha*u,   G_i(v)=alpha^i*H_i(v/alpha).

Then the coefficient of v^16 in G_15 is exactly 1/3 by definition.
The factor three is rational and keeps |alpha| close to one, which helps
conditioning. Numerically,

    alpha = -0.7492155569809897 - 0.6051358964720848 i,
    |alpha| = 0.9630749732089441.

The construction is independent of the arbitrary initial differential
basis. It also cancels a change u -> s*u: the normalized coefficients
acquire factors s^(n-i), and alpha acquires s, so their ratios are
unchanged. Script 74 tests both invariances using a separate synthetic
example with a nontrivial basis change and coordinate rescaling.

CM algebraicity provides the reason to investigate this gauge. The
power-series coefficients of an arithmetic basis have a common period
grading; ratios of equal period weight cancel that grading. The
order-32 sparsity changes the grading by multiples of 32. The jet-basis
normalization and division by alpha^(n-i) follow this pattern. Compare
[Voight–Willis, §2, especially Theorem 2.5 and equations (5)–(7)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1205.0045).
This supplies an algebraicity strategy without first computing a
numerical Hecke eigenbasis. **The field of definition of the normalized
coefficients is not established as F by this calculation.** Being real
does not prove that a floating-point number is algebraic.

## Numerical equations and validation

Extended the expansion from N=8192 to N=10240 using 50-digit geometry
replay and stabilized refinement. The initial shorter basis is padded
with zeros; the new operator is built with 20480 sampling nodes. The
run took about 87 seconds. Geometry is replayed at high precision, but
FFT and linear solves remain double precision. R^(32N), approximately
1.55e-13, is a geometric scale and not a certified error bound.

The independent script 70 audit checks all 275 nonrotational side
actions with 50-digit evaluation geometry. Its largest relative
differential residual is 7.18e-13. Products have numerical rank 45,
with singular values 45 and 46 approximately 0.471 and 9.13e-15.
Linear multiples of the 91 quadrics have rank 741, leaving the expected
75-dimensional degree-three quotient. These results are stored in
`differential_audit_10240.*`. All 34 repository tests, Python compilation,
and `git diff --check` pass.

In the fixed gauge, the weighted series are real up to relative imaginary
norm 1.86e-11. First-64 normalized jets from the two truncations differ
by relative 4.87e-10. Script 74 chooses 45 pivot products and expresses
the other 91 products in terms of them, giving an unambiguous equation
normalization: one nonpivot monomial has coefficient one in each equation.

The residuals and coefficient differences measure different things.
Tiny relation residuals do not imply fifteen accurate digits in every
equation coefficient; the pivot condition number explains the more
modest coefficient stability. These are empirical diagnostics, not
interval enclosures.

`canonical_gauge.npz` stores the 136-by-91 real coefficient array, the
monomial index list, pivot and free monomials, normalized jets, and alpha.
Rows correspond to X_i X_j with i<=j and no factor of two. Variables
refer to the G_i above. Numerical row weights are used only to condition
the linear solve; they do not change the exact relation space.

## Bounded recognition pilot and next work

Script 75 tested six nontrivial normalized jet coefficients. None matched
a rational of denominator at most 1000 within the empirical tolerance.
None produced a PSLQ relation with coefficient height at most four in

    [1,b,b²,...,b^7,value], b=2*cos(7*pi/16).

This is a small bounded search, not evidence that the coefficients are
transcendental or outside F. The input coefficients have about 9–11
empirically stable digits; increasing mpmath's working precision does
not add information to them. No candidate was promoted to an exact value.

Next: obtain substantially higher-precision local jets, preferably with
a structured or multi-center arbitrary-precision solver rather than
indefinite growth of a dense double-precision matrix; then recognize a
field of definition and coefficients with independent validation. Only
61 exact local coefficients are needed for the quadratic relation space,
though computing those accurately may require much longer global
expansions. The general canonical-model route remains open.

Scripts 74–76 and their JSON/NPZ/logs are reproducible via `pari/README.md`.
No algebraic curve model, Jacobian torsion computation, target field
polynomial, external message, or commit was produced in this step.
