# Exact arithmetic input for the CM-normalized functions (D57)

Date: 2026-09-16. This completes the first task in the D56 handoff: audit
actual arithmetic input for the functions constructed in
[cm-pointed-maps.md](cm-pointed-maps.md), before another precision run.

**Subsequent result:** [D58](marked-descent.md) establishes compatible
marked descent using Dembélé's Lemma 5.2 and Sijsling--Voight. That lemma
was overlooked in this audit. Subject to D56's existing hypotheses,
X,Y and their CM polynomials are Q-defined. This supersedes the descent
availability remarks below; the missing exact evaluations remain missing.

**Result:** the CM residue algebra has a concrete exact presentation and
usable reductions. It does **not** supply the evaluations of D56's sections.
The missing input can now be specified as two nested evaluation subspaces
in that algebra. Script [94](../pari/94_cm_arithmetic.py) verifies the
connection to the saved elliptic generators, computes exact reductions,
and gives explicit counterexamples to recovering the values from the field
and the five trace conditions alone. No target value has been recognized.

## 1. Tie the saved CM points to the exact field

Put F=Q(b), with b^8-8b^6+20b^4-16b^2+2=0. The script reloads
`normalizer_domain.bin`; it does not recompute a domain. For all seventeen
order-two elliptic generators q, it verifies exactly

    Tr(q)=0,  q^2=-Nrd(q),
    Tr(q)^2-4*Nrd(q)=(-2-b)*s^2.

In the saved representatives the same witness works for all seventeen:

    s=-4*b^6+22*b^4-28*b^2+4*b+2.

The script also rechecks the scalar identity in Sage, independently of
PARI's square-root calculation. These are the generator indices used by
script 67 to export the CM geometry. Thus the points used in D56 come
from embeddings of K=F(sqrt(-2-b)); this is an exact identification of
their quadratic CM field, not an evaluation of any function on C.

[Dembélé, sections 5.2 and 5.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.06772) give
h(K)=17 and the optimal-embedding count. We retain that class-number
input from the paper; this audit does not rerun its proof. With
beta=i*(zeta_64+zeta_64^-1), the script verifies that K/Q is cyclic of
degree 16, generated by beta -> beta^3+3*beta, whose eighth power is
beta -> -beta. A separate cyclotomic-field test verifies this description.

Here is the class-field consequence, separated from the paper's disputed
hyperellipticity argument. CM reciprocity makes the seventeen points a
transitive class-group orbit over K. Since h(F)=1, conjugation acts by
inversion on Cl(K). If the generator of Gal(K/Q)=C16 acts by a on C17,
then a^8=-1 modulo 17, so a has order 16. Consequently the Hilbert class
field H_K is Galois over Q with group C17 semidirect C16 and is ramified
only above 2. Its identification with the Harbater field supplies the
degree-17 presentation below. Over F the group is D17; the stabilizer of
one CM point is a reflection. Thus D is a closed point of degree 17 over
F, and its residue field is a reflection-fixed subfield of H_K.

This argument uses CM reciprocity and the published class-field
identification. The script does not independently certify those theorems
or construct a labelled isomorphism between number-field embeddings and
the seventeen saved analytic points.

## 2. The available algebra and its actual reductions

Let theta be a root of Elkies's polynomial

    H(t)=t^17-2*t^16+8*t^13+16*t^12-16*t^11+64*t^9-32*t^8
         -80*t^7+32*t^6+40*t^5+80*t^4+16*t^3-128*t^2-2*t+68.

Its original source is [Elkies's MathOverflow answer](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/172148/degree-17-number-fields-ramified-only-at-2);
it is also reproduced in Dembélé's Remark 6.2. We can use

    A_D = H^0(D,O_D) isomorphic to F[t]/H(t)

as an abstract exact arithmetic input. This isomorphism is not a claim
that X(Q)=theta or that H is the CM value polynomial from script 92.
Indeed, the latter polynomial remains unknown exactly.

Exact computations give signature (1,8) and discriminant 2^79 for
Q(theta). The power order has index 23126202879577982; its discriminant
is 2^81 * 11563101439788991^2. In particular, the polynomial's power
basis cannot automatically be used at every odd prime even though the
number field is unramified there.

| Rational prime p | Splitting of K/F at the eight degree-one places | Factor degrees of H modulo p |
|---|---|---|
| 31 | split at all eight | 17 |
| 97 | inert at all eight | 1, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2 |
| 127 | inert at all eight | 1, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2 |
| 191 | inert at all eight | 1, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2 |

The script checks the roots of F's polynomial, the quadratic splitting,
and the displayed factors. Irreducibility at a degree-one prime over 31
also proves H irreducible over F. These reductions agree with D41's
class-field predictions, but still give no reduction of X or Y.

At 31 the available algebra is F_(31^17); at 97 it is
F_97 times eight copies of F_(97^2). Their trace operations are usable
now. Tests compare the multiplication-matrix trace with Frobenius sums
in the field case and with the sum of factor traces in the product case.
The average trace divides by 17, so characteristic 17 is excluded.

## 3. An exact demonstration of the missing information

Write E=Tr_(A_D/F)/17. Starting with (theta,theta^2), D56's prescription
gives the exact pair

    X1=(17*theta-2)/30,        Y1=(2*theta-theta^2)/9.

Starting instead with (theta^2,theta^3) gives

    X2=(17*theta^2-4)/213,
    Y2=8*theta^3/437-8*theta^2/57+32/1311.

Both satisfy all five conditions

    E(X)=E(Y)=E(XY)=0,  E(X^2)=E(X^3),  E(Y^2)=E(Y^3).

But E(X1^2)=16/225 and E(X2^2)=-32/5041. Their degree-17 minimal
polynomials differ, so even relabelling the conjugates cannot identify
the two choices. Reducing the second moments modulo 31 gives 2 and 13;
modulo 97 it gives 85 and 43. Thus the same exact finite residue algebra
and Frobenius information admit distinct normalized spectra too.

These pairs are **counterexamples in the algebra**, not proposed
functions with poles 17P and 18P on C. Their failure to identify the
target says nothing against D56's uniqueness once the actual
Riemann–Roch spaces are supplied. It isolates the missing geometric
input instead of assigning arbitrary algebra generators to X and Y.

For comparison, D56's numerical data give

    E(X^2) approximately 0.39041414756552571896,

with checkpoint change 6.24e-13 and same-checkpoint fit change 5.57e-18.
Neither example is close. These comparisons remain empirical; the smaller
fit change is not an independent error bound. The first exact example
also has denominators at 3 and 5, illustrating why ramification only at 2
does not imply integrality of trace-normalized coordinates away from 2.

## 4. The reconstruction target

Inside the *specified* algebra A_D, obtain the exact flag

    <1> subset V17=ev_D L(17P) subset V18=ev_D L(18P) subset A_D.

Under D56's ordinarity and nonzero-moment hypotheses the dimensions are
1,2,3. Nonzero M2 implies that x does not restrict to a constant;
nonzero N2 implies that y does not restrict to an affine function of x.
Thus restriction is injective on these two spaces. No additional
injectivity hypothesis is needed after those D56 hypotheses, which
remain uncertified on the actual curve.

Given this flag, choose x in V17 outside <1> and y in V18 outside V17,
then apply the exact trace operations already implemented. Changes of
generators are precisely the triangular changes eliminated in D56.
This recovers X|D and Y|D. A flag modulo a good prime, with the necessary
moments nonzero and ranks preserved, similarly yields their reductions.

The field alone does not specify the flag. Abstractly there is a
29-dimensional family of flags: choose a line in A_D/<1> (dimension
15 of choices), then a second direction modulo that line (dimension
14). This counts possible flags in the algebra, **not** pointed curves
having the required global pole behavior. The curve imposes additional
conditions which the residue algebra does not record.

Even recognizing this flag would recover restrictions, not an entire
curve model. Global section multiplication or validated global relations
would still be needed. This is nevertheless a precise arithmetic target
for checking the stable section-ratio construction of scripts 91–92.

## 5. Availability audit

| Input inspected | What it supplies | What remains missing for this target |
|---|---|---|
| Saved exact quaternion generators and CM reciprocity | CM field, orbit and abstract residue algebra | Section evaluations in that algebra |
| Saved seventeen complex CM evaluations | One full CM orbit over the chosen embedding of F | Exact values or the other seven F-embeddings of the traces |
| Integral Hecke/Brandt matrices and Frobenius polynomials | Cohomology and arithmetic permutations | Multiplication and evaluation of canonical sections |
| Stable graph at 2 | Combinatorial degeneration and monodromy | A marked formal model with deformation and section data |
| D56 section ratios | Numerical representatives of the desired spaces | Exact arithmetic multiplication/evaluation tensors |
| User's Belyi/thesis code | Exact controls and function construction on known models | A function-field or arithmetic evaluation input for C |

Permuting the seventeen CM points fixes every F-valued trace. It cannot
produce the other embeddings of such a trace. A compatible descent of
the **marked** objects to Q could help, but no such descent maps have
been supplied or certified here; an unmarked descent assertion is not
used to declare these particular values rational.

Nor do the available digits supply a height bound. There are arbitrarily
small nonzero elements of F at the chosen real embedding with large
other conjugates. Perturbations in the abstract flag can therefore be
arbitrarily small at this embedding while having unbounded arithmetic
height. No rational/integer-recognition search on these examples is a
substitute for controlling the actual flag's height and denominators.

The CM-value literature was also checked. [Guo–Yang](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1510.06193)
construct particular Borcherds forms and use CM norms to obtain equations
over Q. [Bruinier–Yang](https://www.mathematik.tu-darmstadt.de/media/algebra/homepages/bruinier/publikationen/shim-cm7b.pdf),
Theorem 1.1 and section 7, provides a totally real version using
Whittaker-form input and special divisors. These are real sources of
arithmetic information, but applying them requires a specified lift and
divisor. No such representation of D56's X,Y or section ratios has
been constructed. Norm formulas for specified functions are not an
oracle for additive traces of unspecified functions. In particular,
D56's rejected proposal div(x-c)=D-17P does not provide the required
CM-supported principal divisor. This audit does not exclude constructing
different suitable functions by those methods.

**Decision:** no available inspected input currently evaluates even one
of the target traces independently and exactly. Do not launch another
large analytic solve merely because the residue field is known. The
next arithmetic construction must deliver the flag above (or exact
section multiplication/evaluation data that determine it), or construct
a specific special-divisor function with an applicable CM-value formula.
The prime-31 algebra and the current section ratios give a concrete
starting interface; a new geometric evaluation method is still needed.
This is an input gap, not a proof that the curve route cannot work.
