## Expansion and geometry

Script 67 exports 57-digit disk actions from Rickards's saved domain and
conjugates the order-32 elliptic fixed point to the origin. Its position in
the original disk is approximately

    0.37609510095471238 + 0.16333792061494643 i.

The conjugated elliptic off-diagonal entry is below 1e-57. A differential
in the new coordinate has the sparse expansion

    omega = w^31 H(w^32) dw.

The coarse coordinate at this elliptic point is u=w^32, so equivalently
omega=H(u) du/32. Sparsity reduces the unknowns by a factor of 32.
The saved coefficient convention is

    H(w^32) = sum_j c_j (w/R)^(32j),  R=0.99991.

Script 69 reconstructs a CM-centered Dirichlet sector using side actions
and all 32 rotations. It finds 265 vertices, maximum radius
0.9999083389704847, and area/(2 pi)=45.46874998525, agreeing with 1455/32
within 1.5e-8. This is finite-precision geometric evidence, not a certified
polygon enclosure. The sampling radius is just outside this sector.

Script 68 applies the Cauchy/Fourier power-series method. If v=g(w), then

    H(w^32) = g'(w) (v/w)^31 H(v^32).

For N coefficients, 2N samples yield a matrix A with c approximately Ac.
This follows the framework of
[Voight–Willis, Computing power series expansions of modular forms](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1205.0045).
The user's local Belyi implementation, especially
`/Users/musty/Belyi/Code/powser_iter_arfed.m` and
`/Users/musty/Belyi/Cext/powser_arnoldi.c`, was also inspected for its
multi-center and Arnoldi approach. Its triangle-group geometry was not
used as a drop-in replacement for this polygon.

The final refinement replays reduction words with 50-digit arithmetic,
using the exported 57-digit actions. Its hyperbolic metric consistency
error is below 2.9e-43 before conversion to double precision. FFT and
linear algebra remain double precision. Given the initial 16-column basis
B, solve

    (I-A+B B*) C = B,

then orthonormalize C. The completed refinement took about 50 seconds on
this machine, including geometry replay and matrix construction. The
independent audit evaluates the original SU(1,1) fractional-linear formula
with 50-digit arithmetic at 275 separate overlap points, followed by double
precision polynomial evaluation.

## Failed pilots and safeguards

- R=0.9998 does not cover the quotient. It produces extra approximate
  kernel directions even when local residuals look small. Increasing N
  increased the number of spurious directions. These pilots are retained
  but must not be used as a basis.
- N=512 and N=2048 at R=0.99999 did not resolve the expected space.
- N=4096 at the adequate radius isolates exactly 16 directions; N=8192
  improves accuracy substantially.
- Single-vector ARPACK resolves the slightly split unit eigenvalues in
  ordinary precision, but finds only seven of the almost coincident unit
  directions after high-precision geometry replay. Selecting its first
  16 columns then gives an invalid basis. `8192_mp` is a failed pilot.
- Ordinary block power iteration is also invalid here: A has unwanted
  eigenvalues of modulus greater than one. `8192_block` diverges and is
  retained only as a failed diagnostic. Stabilized refinement replaces it.
- R^(32N), about 5.67e-11 at N=8192, is a geometric scale only. It is not a
  Taylor-tail bound. Likewise the largest scaled reduced monomial is not
  an error estimate.

## Hyperellipticity discrepancy and local-coordinate audit

[Dembélé, Theorem 5.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.06772) states that this
quotient is hyperelliptic. Such a genus-16 curve has product dimension
2g-1=31, not 45. Script 70 includes a synthetic hyperelliptic control with
a nonlinear local coordinate and common differential factor; it gives
rank 31 as expected. The observed gap at rank 45 is stable and much larger
than the numerical error.

There is a specific concern in the printed proof. Its opening step uses
an order-two element of the uniformizing group to infer even vanishing
orders of differentials on the quotient. Here is our local audit, which
does not depend on the numerical solver:

An elliptic stabilizer acts on an **upstairs** parameter t by t -> -t.
The invariant local ring is C[[u]], u=t^2. The stabilizer acts trivially
on this quotient ring. An invariant differential upstairs has the form

    t h(t^2) dt = (1/2) h(u) du.

Its order on the quotient is ord_u(h), with no evenness requirement.
For example, u du vanishes to order one downstairs and pulls back to
2t^3 dt, which is invariant upstairs. Thus stabilizer invariance alone
does not give the equality l(K-2P)=l(K-P) used in that argument. This
identifies an apparent gap in that step; it does not by itself disprove
the theorem or constitute a certified nonhyperellipticity proof.

The paper's Proposition 6.5 and second proof of Theorem 6.4 provide a
separate induced-character argument for the F16 residual systems. This
local-coordinate concern does not automatically reopen D40 as a
nonsolvable target. We have not independently certified all steps of
that second proof. No external messages were sent about this discrepancy.

## Saved section data and next gate

`differential_audit.npz` contains the basis transformation and 91 columns
of quadratic relations. Variables refer to the 16 differentials after
applying `basis_transform` on the right. Rows of `quadrics` are indexed by
(i,j) in lexicographic order with 0<=i<=j<16, with monomial X_i X_j and no
extra factor of two. Relations were fitted to the first 256 product
coefficients and tested on coefficients 256 through 511.

The first 16 differential jets have condition number about 555. This
allows a local jet normalization, but it does **not** make coefficients
algebraic: disk coordinates include analytic period scaling. These
complex numerical quadrics cannot simply be reduced modulo 97.
