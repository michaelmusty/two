# Exact verification of the CM-frame curve (D69)

Date: 2026-09-18.

**Result:** the 91 integer quadrics from D68 define a **smooth,
geometrically integral, canonically embedded genus-16 curve over Q**.
It has good reduction at 31. Direct point counts on that reduction give
50 points over F31 and 1,400 over F31², agreeing with the two counts
predicted by the saved genus-16 Hecke data.

**Still open:** identification with the specified Shimura curve C.
Smoothness and these two point counts do not prove that identification.
The degree-three cover Y/C, target torsion, and Dembélé field polynomial
have not been constructed. This report upgrades the algebraic properties
of D68's candidate; it does not certify its numerical discovery step.

The equations themselves are unchanged:
[Magma model](../data/computed/cm_frame_curve_candidate.m),
[JSON coefficients](../data/computed/cm_frame_curve_candidate.json).
See [D68](curve-construction-sources-2026-09-18.md) for the projective-frame
construction and its inherited descent/orbit qualifications.

## 1. A finite free projection replaces Jacobian minors

Use the Magma coordinate names X1,...,X16. Let I be the homogeneous
ideal generated by the quadrics. Its reduced Gröbner basis has 105
elements. None of its leading monomials involves X15 or X16. The
standard monomials in the first fourteen variables are

    1;
    X14, X13, ..., X1;
    X14^2, X13*X14, ..., X1*X14;
    X14^3.

Thus the homogeneous coordinate ring is a free module of rank 30
over k[X15,X16], with basis degrees `0, 1^14, 2^14, 3`. The same
statement is checked over Q and F31. In particular X15 and X16 have
no common zero on the projective curve, and define a finite flat map
of degree 30 to P1. This map is an auxiliary projection, not the
sought degree-three cover and not an identified Belyi map.

On the chart X16=1, write t=X15. The thirty monomials give a basis
of the affine coordinate algebra B over A=F31[t]. Normal-form reduction
computes every product of two basis elements. From these products the
script computes the matrix

    H_ij(t) = Tr_(B/A)(b_i*b_j),     d(t) = det H(t).

Here the trace is the trace of multiplication on the actual rank-30
algebra. We do not substitute the discriminant of an unverified
primitive-element polynomial. The construction is the finite locally
free trace discriminant of the
[Stacks Project, §49.3](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BVH).

Exact results over F31:

| Check | Result |
|---|---:|
| Degree of d(t) | 90 |
| gcd(d,d') | 1 |
| Constant / leading coefficient | 1 / 16 |
| Degrees of irreducible factors | 3, 9, 25, 53 |
| Discriminant at infinity | 16, nonzero |

The second chart X15=1 is computed separately. Its discriminant is
exactly `u^90*d(1/u)`, is also squarefree, and is nonzero at u=0.
The complete coefficient list is retained in the
[verification JSON](../data/computed/cm_frame_curve_verification.json)
and [smoothness log](../data/computed/cm_frame_smoothness.out).

## 2. Why this proves smoothness

Here is the elementary index argument used by the certificate. Suppose
B is a finite free domain over k[t], with nonzero trace discriminant d.
Its fraction algebra is separable. Its integral closure Bbar is finite
over k[t] and is free of the same rank. At each prime of k[t], choose
bases and let M express a basis of B in a basis of Bbar. Then

    disc(B) = det(M)^2 * disc(Bbar).

All factors are integral at that prime. If d is squarefree, the
valuation of det(M) must be zero at every prime. Hence B=Bbar: B is
normal. A normal curve over a perfect field is smooth; see
[Stacks, Lemma 33.43.8](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0B8Y).
The argument only supplies a sufficient condition: repeated factors
in d need not mean that the source curve is singular.

The special fiber is checked to be reduced and irreducible, so its
affine chart rings are domains. The index argument on both charts
therefore proves smoothness everywhere. The rational point

    [0,17,29,28,7,14,2,2,0,8,8,13,0,7,0,1]

lies on this special fiber exactly. An integral smooth curve over a
finite field with a rational point is geometrically integral.

For characteristic zero, the monic Gröbner basis over Q has no
coefficient denominator divisible by 31. Its reduction is exactly
the reduced Gröbner basis of the original quadrics modulo 31. The
basis therefore defines a flat projective model over Z_(31), with
the asserted generic and special fibers. The model meant here is
the one defined by that monic basis; no extra flatness assumption
about an arbitrary integral presentation is needed.

Flatness and smoothness of the special fiber make the morphism smooth
along that fiber, by
[Stacks, Lemma 29.35.14](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01V4).
The nonsmooth locus is closed. Its image under the proper morphism
is closed and misses the closed point of Spec Z_(31), hence is empty.
The generic curve over Q is smooth as well.

The graded free basis also gives, over either field and after extending
that field,

    pi_* O_C = O + O(-1)^14 + O(-2)^14 + O(-3).

Consequently h0(O_C)=1 and h0(O_C(1))=16. The former gives geometric
connectedness, and smoothness then gives geometric integrality. The
Hilbert polynomial `30*n-15` gives genus 16 and degree O_C(1)=30.
Riemann–Roch gives h0(K_C-O_C(1))=1. This line bundle has degree zero,
so it is trivial. Thus the exhibited embedding is canonical. This
conclusion is about the candidate itself and requires no conjectural
identification with the Shimura curve.

## 3. Controls and independent point counts

[Magma script 117](../magma/117_cm_frame_smoothness.m) checks its trace
construction on three small plane curves over F31:

* `x^2-y*z`: a smooth conic, with discriminant `4*t` on either chart.
* `x^3-y^2*z`: a cuspidal cubic, with discriminant `-27*t^4`; the
  squarefree criterion fails, and the ordinary singularity test agrees.
* `x^3+y^3+z^3`: a smooth cubic whose discriminant is
  `-27*(t^3+1)^2`; this verifies that rejection by the criterion does
  not get reported as singularity.

For the target, multiplication matrices on the fibers t=0,1,2 and
at infinity independently reproduce all 465 distinct entries of the
corresponding trace matrices. The script rechecks the rational Gröbner
model, both chart discriminants, integrality and a rational point.

[Magma script 118](../magma/118_cm_frame_point_counts.m) enumerates
the rational points of each degree-30 fiber of this projection as a
zero-dimensional scheme. It verifies every returned point against
the original 91 quadrics and its fiber equation. Over F31² it uses
F31-Frobenius orbits of base values: 496 affine orbits plus infinity.
This is 497 distinct fiber calculations, representing all 962 base
points. The F31 calculation has 32 fibers. Neither count uses the
expected value to find points.

The four quartic factors of the saved level-p2-new T31 polynomial
are the genus-16 quotient constituents selected in scripts 60 and 64.
Their product is P16. The predicted degree-32 Frobenius polynomial is

    Phi31(U) = U^16 * P16(U + 31/U).

Its first two power traces are -18 and -438. The comparison is:

| Field | Direct curve count | Hecke prediction |
|---|---:|---:|
| F31 | 50 | 50 |
| F31² | 1,400 | 1,400 |

The comparison is at the single rational prime 31, which splits
completely in F. It is not two distinct-prime comparisons, not the
full Euler polynomial, and not an isomorphism or modularity proof.
The exact counts and per-fiber records are in the verification JSON
and [degree-one](../data/computed/cm_frame_points31_degree1.out) /
[degree-two](../data/computed/cm_frame_points31_degree2.out) logs.

## 4. Reproduction and next identification criterion

From the repository root:

```sh
python3 dembele/pari/117_cm_frame_verification.py
python3 dembele/pari/117_cm_frame_verification.py --collect-only
```

The wrapper uses the Python standard library and local Magma. Each of
the three jobs has a 180-second timeout and 2 GB memory cap. It requires
the final PASS marker, zero exit status and absence of Magma error
messages. It records source/log hashes and timings. Collection checks
the hashes and recomputes totals from the saved fiber records. No
normalization run or full Jacobian-minor enumeration is used.
The recorded wall times are 4.655 s for the smoothness job, 5.140 s
for the F31 count, and 82.171 s for the F31² count. All three jobs
and collection pass. The six D68–D69 provenance reports validate
43 distinct source/log hashes; `git diff --check` also passes.

A sufficient identification criterion would be to verify, on the
**specified Shimura curve**, exact vanishing through order three of
these quadrics at the degree-17 CM point, in the matching arithmetic
canonical coordinates. A quadratic canonical section has degree 60;
order at least four at that closed point imposes degree at least
`4*17=68>60`, so such a section is zero. This is the nonrational-point
vanishing bound of
[van Bommel–Costa–Poonen–Srinivasan, Corollary 4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.14026).
The canonical image would then lie on this smooth degree-30 curve;
the degree of the canonical bundle is also 30, so the resulting map
has degree one and identifies the curves.

The missing input is an independent exact arithmetic determination of
those Shimura-side jets, or another sufficient identification theorem.
The earlier discrepancy with the published hyperellipticity assertion
is not resolved by proving properties of this candidate.
Computing jets from the candidate's own equations would be circular.
The saved complex banks and their small residuals do not establish
exact vanishing. More point counts can strengthen evidence but do not
replace this proof obligation. The next research step is to obtain
independent arithmetic local data with that criterion in view, then
construct the actual Y/C. Downstream backend optimization stays paused.

D68's numerical banks, equations, hashes and failed probes are preserved.
The new work uses local files only. No target torsion, remote job,
external checkout edit, message, commit or cleanup is part of D69.
